How to use this formulary
Each entry lists the HVMC formulary code (HV-RX-####), drug class, formulary status, indications, usual adult dosing, renal dose adjustment, key interactions, monitoring and pharmacist notes. Renal adjustments use creatinine clearance (CrCl) calculated with the Cockcroft-Gault equation and actual body weight. This formulary covers adults only; consult the separate pediatric handbook for patients under 18. Non-formulary requests are submitted to pharmacy and reviewed within 24 hours.

HV-RX-2010 Heparivex (low molecular weight heparin)
Formulary status: Tier 1, unrestricted.
Indications: Prevention of venous thromboembolism (VTE) in hospitalized adults; treatment of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE).
Adult dosing: VTE prophylaxis: 40 mg subcutaneously once daily. Treatment: 1 mg/kg subcutaneously every 12 hours.
Renal adjustment: CrCl below 30 mL/min: prophylaxis 30 mg once daily; treatment 1 mg/kg once daily.
Interactions: Additive bleeding risk with antiplatelet agents and Ketrolin.
Monitoring: Platelet count every 2 to 3 days for the first 14 days to detect heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. Anti-Xa level (LC-0920) in patients over 150 kg or with CrCl below 30 mL/min.
Pharmacist notes: Do not expel the air bubble from the prefilled syringe before injection.

HV-RX-2047 Corvatrel (oral factor Xa inhibitor)
Formulary status: Tier 1, unrestricted.
Indications: Stroke prevention in non-valvular atrial fibrillation; treatment of DVT and PE.
Adult dosing: 15 mg by mouth once daily with the evening meal.
Renal adjustment: CrCl 15 to 50 mL/min: 10 mg once daily. CrCl below 15 mL/min: avoid use.
Interactions: Avoid combination with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as Nephrazole, which roughly doubles Corvatrel exposure.
Monitoring: CBC and serum creatinine at baseline, then renal function every 6 months.
Pharmacist notes: No specific reversal agent is stocked. For life-threatening bleeding, contact hematology for four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate.

HV-RX-2074 Corvetrol (oral direct thrombin inhibitor)
Formulary status: Tier 2, pharmacist verification required.
Indications: Stroke prevention in non-valvular atrial fibrillation.
Adult dosing: 110 mg by mouth twice daily. Capsules must not be opened or crushed.
Renal adjustment: CrCl 30 to 50 mL/min: 75 mg twice daily. CrCl below 30 mL/min: contraindicated.
Interactions: P-glycoprotein inhibitors increase Corvetrol levels.
Monitoring: aPTT (LC-0625) may be used as a qualitative indicator of drug presence.
Pharmacist notes: The reversal agent is Revexa (HV-RX-2081).

HV-RX-2081 Revexa (monoclonal antibody fragment reversal agent)
Formulary status: Restricted: requires approval from the hematology or emergency department attending.
Indications: Reversal of Corvetrol before emergency surgery or in life-threatening bleeding.
Adult dosing: 5 g IV, given as two consecutive 2.5 g boluses no more than 15 minutes apart.
Renal adjustment: No adjustment.
Interactions: Does not reverse Corvatrel or Heparivex.
Monitoring: aPTT 2 to 4 hours after administration.
Pharmacist notes: Stored refrigerated in the emergency department automated dispensing cabinet. Restart anticoagulation within 24 hours once hemostasis is achieved.

Section: Anti-infectives

HV-RX-1102 Zephacillin (extended-spectrum penicillin with beta-lactamase inhibitor)
Formulary status: Tier 1; infectious diseases (ID) approval required to continue beyond 72 hours.
Indications: Empiric therapy for sepsis of unknown source, hospital-acquired pneumonia and intra-abdominal infection.
Adult dosing: 4.5 g IV every 6 hours. The first dose is given over 30 minutes; later doses run as an extended infusion over 4 hours, which is the HVMC standard.
Renal adjustment: CrCl 20 to 40 mL/min: 3.375 g every 6 hours. CrCl below 20 mL/min: 2.25 g every 6 hours.
Interactions: Increased nephrotoxicity when combined with Vancorin.
Monitoring: Serum creatinine daily; CBC twice weekly.
Pharmacist notes: First-line agent in the sepsis order set (CP-07).

HV-RX-1108 Cefalyn (third-generation cephalosporin)
Formulary status: Tier 1, unrestricted.
Indications: Community-acquired pneumonia, pyelonephritis and bacterial meningitis.
Adult dosing: 2 g IV once daily. Bacterial meningitis: 2 g IV every 12 hours.
Renal adjustment: No adjustment.
Interactions: Do not co-administer with calcium-containing IV solutions in the same line.
Monitoring: CBC weekly; watch for biliary sludge with prolonged courses.
Pharmacist notes: Cefalyn plus Azithrel is the standard ward regimen for community-acquired pneumonia.

HV-RX-1120 Meronex (carbapenem)
Formulary status: Restricted: ID approval required before the first dose, except in septic shock, where approval may be obtained within 24 hours.
Indications: Infections with ESBL-producing organisms and severe nosocomial infections.
Adult dosing: 1 g IV every 8 hours. Central nervous system infections: 2 g IV every 8 hours.
Renal adjustment: CrCl 26 to 50 mL/min: 1 g every 12 hours. CrCl 10 to 25 mL/min: 500 mg every 12 hours.
Interactions: Lowers valproic acid levels by up to 90 percent; avoid the combination and choose an alternative antiepileptic.
Monitoring: Seizure precautions in patients with central nervous system disease.
Pharmacist notes: Reserve for documented resistance to protect the hospital antibiogram.

HV-RX-1135 Vancorin (glycopeptide antibiotic with MRSA coverage)
Formulary status: Tier 1; dosed by pharmacy under the pharmacy-to-dose protocol.
Indications: Suspected or confirmed MRSA infection; empiric sepsis coverage together with Zephacillin.
Adult dosing: Loading dose 25 mg/kg IV (maximum 3,000 mg), then maintenance dosing targeting an AUC of 400 to 600. Infuse no faster than 1 g per hour.
Renal adjustment: Pharmacy adjusts the dose using drug levels.
Interactions: Nephrotoxicity is increased with Zephacillin and Ketrolin.
Monitoring: Drug levels per LC-0910; serum creatinine daily.
Pharmacist notes: Flushing of the upper body during a fast infusion is an infusion reaction, not an allergy: slow the rate.

HV-RX-1150 Azithrel (macrolide antibiotic)
Formulary status: Tier 1, unrestricted.
Indications: Community-acquired pneumonia (atypical coverage).
Adult dosing: 500 mg IV or by mouth once daily for 3 days.
Renal adjustment: No adjustment.
Interactions: QT prolongation with Lumidex, Halopran and Tarnaflox; obtain an ECG if combined.
Monitoring: Baseline QTc in patients with cardiac disease.
Pharmacist notes: Switch from IV to oral once the patient tolerates oral intake.

HV-RX-1172 Tarnaflox (fluoroquinolone)
Formulary status: Restricted to patients with a documented beta-lactam allergy or to susceptibility-guided therapy.
Indications: Pneumonia and complicated urinary tract infection.
Adult dosing: 750 mg IV or by mouth once daily.
Renal adjustment: CrCl 20 to 49 mL/min: 750 mg every 48 hours.
Interactions: Oral absorption is reduced by antacids, iron and magnesium; separate doses by at least 2 hours. Additive QT prolongation.
Monitoring: Ask about tendon pain at every assessment.
Pharmacist notes: Boxed warning for tendon rupture, higher in patients over 60 and in those taking corticosteroids such as Prednavex. Avoid in myasthenia gravis.
