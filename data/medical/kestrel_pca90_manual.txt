Kestrel PCA-90 Patient-Controlled Analgesia Pump
Operator and Troubleshooting Manual, HVMC edition, revision 5
SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT: fictional facility, drugs and devices, created for retrieval testing. Not medical guidance.

1. Overview
The Kestrel PCA-90 lets a patient give themselves a small, preset dose of opioid by pressing a handset button, within limits set by the prescriber. At HVMC it is used with Hydrovane and Morphalin in prefilled 30 mL cassettes. Only the patient may press the button; family members and staff must never press it for them ("PCA by proxy"), because sedation is the safety signal that stops further doses.

2. Programming parameters
Each PCA order specifies a demand dose, a lockout interval, an optional continuous (basal) rate and a 1-hour or 4-hour dose limit. The lockout interval is the minimum time between doses; button presses during lockout are recorded but not delivered. Continuous basal infusions are not used for opioid-naive patients at HVMC. Two nurses must independently verify the programming against the order at initial setup, at every cassette change and at every handover.

3. The security key
The cassette door is locked with a security key held by the charge nurse. A missing key must be reported to pharmacy within the same shift. Wasting the remaining opioid in a cassette requires a second nurse as witness.

4. Monitoring
Assess the sedation score (POSS) and respiratory rate every hour for the first 12 hours, then every 2 hours. Continuous pulse oximetry is required. If the patient is difficult to rouse, stop the PCA and follow protocol CP-15.

5. Fault codes
Codes on the PCA-90 have their own meanings and must not be read from the IV-220 manual.

E-01 Cassette not locked
Type: clinical, high priority.
Meaning: The cassette door is unlocked.
Action: Lock the door with the security key held by the charge nurse.

E-03 Handset disconnected
Type: clinical, medium priority.
Meaning: The patient handset cable is unplugged.
Action: Reconnect the handset and check that the patient can reach it.

E-05 Cassette low
Type: clinical, medium priority.
Meaning: Less than 5 mL remains in the cassette.
Action: Request a new cassette from pharmacy.

E-06 Cassette empty
Type: clinical, high priority.
Meaning: The cassette is empty; delivery has stopped.
Action: Change the cassette with a second nurse verifying the programming.

E-08 Dose limit reached
Type: clinical, medium priority.
Meaning: The 1-hour or 4-hour dose limit has been reached; demand doses are blocked.
Action: Assess pain and sedation; contact the prescriber if pain is uncontrolled. Do not raise the limit without an order.

E-12 Patient line occlusion
Type: clinical, high priority.
Meaning: The tubing to the patient is blocked.
Action: Check the clamp and the IV site, then restart.

E-17 Excess demand presses
Type: clinical, low priority.
Meaning: More than 10 button presses occurred during one lockout interval.
Action: Assess pain control; frequent presses during lockout usually mean the regimen is inadequate. Notify the prescriber.

E-19 Programming not verified
Type: clinical, high priority.
Meaning: The second-nurse verification step was skipped.
Action: Complete independent double verification before starting.

E-24 Security key left in door
Type: clinical, low priority.
Meaning: The key has remained in the cassette lock for more than 2 minutes.
Action: Remove the key and return it to the charge nurse.

E-52 Pump door forced
Type: clinical, high priority.
Meaning: The locked cassette door was opened without the key.
Action: Treat as a possible diversion event: secure the pump, notify the charge nurse and pharmacy, and report in the safety event system.

E-61 Handset button stuck
Type: technical.
Meaning: The handset reports a continuous press.
Action: Technical fault. Replace the handset; if the code persists, remove the pump from service.

E-62 Delivery mechanism fault
Type: technical.
Meaning: The pumping mechanism did not move as commanded.
Action: Technical fault. Remove from service.

E-71 Event log full
Type: technical.
Meaning: The pump history memory is full and older events cannot be overwritten.
Action: Technical fault. Remove from service so biomedical engineering can download the log for opioid accountability.


Out-of-service procedure
Attach a red "DO NOT USE" tag with the fault code and asset tag and call biomedical engineering at extension 4417. For any device holding a controlled substance, the remaining drug is wasted with a witness before the device leaves the unit.
