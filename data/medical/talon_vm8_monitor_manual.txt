Talon VM-8 Bedside Patient Monitor
Quick Reference and Alarm Guide, HVMC edition
SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT: fictional facility, drugs and devices, created for retrieval testing. Not medical guidance.

1. Overview
The Talon VM-8 displays ECG, heart rate, non-invasive blood pressure, oxygen saturation (SpO2), respiratory rate, temperature and, when the module is fitted, end-tidal carbon dioxide (EtCO2). Every inpatient bed on the telemetry, step-down and ICU units has a VM-8 that reports to the central station at the nurses' desk. Monitors on general wards are portable and do not report centrally unless docked.

2. Default alarm limits for adults
Heart rate: low 50, high 120 beats per minute. SpO2: low 90 percent. Respiratory rate: low 8, high 30 breaths per minute. Systolic blood pressure: low 90, high 180 mmHg. EtCO2: low 25, high 50 mmHg. Limits may be changed only with a provider order or under a unit protocol, and changes must be documented. Alarm limits reset to the defaults when a new patient is admitted to the monitor.

3. Reducing nuisance alarms
Change ECG electrodes every 24 hours after cleaning the skin with soap and water. Place the SpO2 sensor on a warm finger without nail polish. Use the correct cuff size: the bladder should cover 80 percent of the arm circumference. Never turn alarm volume below the unit minimum or disable an alarm category.

4. Alarm messages

Alarm ASYSTOLE
Priority: red, highest priority.
Meaning: No QRS complexes detected for 4 seconds.
Action: Check the patient immediately; start resuscitation if unresponsive and pulseless.

Alarm VFIB/VTACH
Priority: red, highest priority.
Meaning: Ventricular fibrillation or sustained ventricular tachycardia detected.
Action: Check the patient immediately; call a code if pulseless.

Alarm BRADY
Priority: yellow, medium priority.
Meaning: Heart rate below the low limit.
Action: Assess the patient and look for drug causes such as Metavol or Sedoflux.

Alarm TACHY
Priority: yellow, medium priority.
Meaning: Heart rate above the high limit.
Action: Assess for pain, fever, bleeding or arrhythmia.

Alarm SPO2 LOW
Priority: yellow, medium priority.
Meaning: Oxygen saturation below the low limit for 10 seconds.
Action: Check the probe and the patient's breathing; give oxygen per order.

Alarm SPO2 PROBE OFF
Priority: cyan, technical.
Meaning: The oxygen sensor is not on the patient.
Action: Reattach the sensor.

Alarm RR LOW
Priority: yellow, medium priority.
Meaning: Respiratory rate below the low limit.
Action: Assess sedation and breathing; consider opioid-induced respiratory depression (CP-15).

Alarm APNEA
Priority: red, highest priority.
Meaning: No breath detected for 20 seconds.
Action: Stimulate the patient and assess airway and breathing immediately.

Alarm ETCO2 HIGH
Priority: yellow, medium priority.
Meaning: End-tidal carbon dioxide above the high limit.
Action: Assess for hypoventilation, especially in patients on opioids.

Alarm NBP CUFF LEAK
Priority: cyan, technical.
Meaning: The blood pressure cuff did not inflate.
Action: Check the cuff and hose connections.

Alarm LEADS OFF
Priority: cyan, technical.
Meaning: One or more ECG electrodes are disconnected.
Action: Replace electrodes; check skin preparation.

Alarm ARTIFACT
Priority: cyan, technical.
Meaning: Signal noise prevents analysis.
Action: Check electrodes and patient movement.

Alarm BATTERY LOW
Priority: cyan, technical.
Meaning: Less than 20 minutes of battery remain on a portable monitor.
Action: Dock or plug in the monitor.

Alarm NO CENTRAL
Priority: yellow, technical.
Meaning: The monitor has lost its connection to the central station.
Action: Check the network cable or wireless status; inform the charge nurse because alarms are not reaching the desk.

5. Service
Faulty monitors are tagged and reported to biomedical engineering at extension 4417.
